# Earthframe TV · Video 1: Owls, Silent Hunters of the Night

- Hedef: ~8 dk, sakin belgesel anlatımı (ElevenLabs Lily, speed 0.9). Görüntü: Pixabay gerçek çekimler. Telif: `docs/TELIF.md`.
- Bilgi kaynakları: Cornell Lab of Ornithology (All About Birds), Audubon, The Barn Owl Trust, RSPB, Konishi (1973) baykuş işitme deneyleri, Smithsonian.

## Bilgi kontrolü
- 200'den fazla baykuş türü; Antarktika hariç her kıtada.
- Gözler tüp şeklinde, yuvasında dönmez; baş ~270° döner; boyunda 14 omur (insanda 7).
- Yüz diski sesi kulaklara yönlendirir; peçeli baykuşta kulaklar asimetrik (biri yukarıda), tam karanlıkta yalnız sesle avlanabilir.
- Sessiz uçuş: kanat ön kenarında tarak gibi dişler, arka kenarda yumuşak saçaklar, kadifemsi yüzey.
- Avı bütün yutar, sindiremediği kemik ve tüyleri "pelet" olarak geri çıkarır.
- Kar baykuşu Arktik yazında gündüz de avlanır; puhu en büyük baykuşlardan; cüce baykuş (elf owl) serçe boyunda; oyuk baykuşu yer altında yuvalanır.
- Baykuşlar yuva yapmaz (ağaç kovuğu, eski yuvalar, binalar). Bir peçeli baykuş ailesi bir yılda binlerce kemirgen yer.

## Metin (paragraflar = sahneler)
P0: As the sun slips behind the trees, the forest grows quiet. The birds of the day fall silent, one by one. But for some, the day is only beginning. Welcome to Earthframe. Tonight, we follow the silent hunters of the night... the owls.
P1: There are more than two hundred kinds of owls, and they live on every continent except Antarctica. Some are as small as a sparrow. Others are big enough to hunt a hare. But almost all of them share the same set of remarkable tools.
P2: Look into an owl's face, and the first thing you notice are the eyes. They are huge, and they face forward, like ours. But an owl's eyes are not round balls. They are long and tube-shaped, and they cannot move in their sockets at all.
P3: So instead, the owl moves its whole head. An owl can turn its head about two hundred and seventy degrees, looking almost straight behind it. Its neck has fourteen small bones, twice as many as ours, and special blood vessels keep the blood flowing even when the head is turned.
P4: But the owl's greatest secret is not its eyes. It's its ears. The round, flat face of an owl works like a satellite dish. Its feathers gather the faintest sounds, and guide them toward the ears, hidden on the sides of the head.
P5: In some owls, like the barn owl, one ear sits a little higher than the other. A sound reaches each ear at a slightly different moment, and from a slightly different angle. By comparing the two, the owl can find a mouse in total darkness, using sound alone. It can even hear prey moving under leaves, or beneath the snow.
P6: And then, it flies. Without a sound. The front edge of an owl's wing has tiny, comb-like teeth. The back edge is soft, like a fringe. Together, they break up the rushing air, and muffle the noise of each wingbeat. The mouse below never hears it coming.
P7: Owls usually swallow small prey whole. Later, they bring up the parts they can't digest, the bones and fur, packed into a small, neat bundle called a pellet. Scientists study these pellets to learn what owls eat, and which animals live nearby.
P8: Every owl has its own way of life. The snowy owl lives in the Arctic, where summer nights are bright, so it often hunts in daylight. The Eurasian eagle-owl is one of the largest owls in the world, with bright orange eyes. The burrowing owl lives underground, in tunnels dug by other animals. And the tiny elf owl, no heavier than a few coins, nests in holes in desert cacti.
P9: Owls don't build nests of their own. They move into hollow trees, old nests left by other birds, quiet barns, and even church towers. A single family of barn owls can eat thousands of mice and rats in a year, which makes them welcome friends to many farmers.
P10: As the night goes on, the owl calls out across the forest. A soft hoot, to tell others: this place is mine. Then, silence again. Somewhere in the dark, a pair of great eyes keeps watch... and listens.
P11: Thank you for watching Earthframe. Take a slow breath, and enjoy the quiet. We'll see you next time, somewhere in the wild.
