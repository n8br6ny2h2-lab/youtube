## Anlatım metni
One bolt of lightning heats the air to about 30,000 degrees Celsius. That's five times hotter than the surface of the Sun. And it happens in a fraction of a second. The air around the bolt expands so fast that it explodes outward. That explosion is thunder. Here's a trick: when you see a flash, count the seconds until you hear the thunder. Divide by three, and that's how many kilometers away the storm is. Follow Kavci Lab for more.

- Ses havada ~343 m/s: 3 saniye ≈ 1 km, 5 saniye ≈ 1 mil (NWS).
